## Observed facts (this build)

- `cargo test` passes 50/50 in the Soroban host:
  - 47 unit and negative tests;
  - 1 property test, 300 random sequences of up to 60 steps, with every invariant checked after every step;
  - 2 scenario tests: the 44-order seed replayed as 148 calls, and the order-id vectors shared with TypeScript.
- `stellar contract build` produces a 27,763-byte wasm.
- `npm test` passes 72/72 offline. `simulate` reproduces `data/seed/expected.json` exactly.
- In the host, a refund to a deauthorised balance is caught by `try_transfer` and recorded as pending (the host view of item (a); not a testnet observation).
- In the property test, a compromised arbiter never moved funds anywhere except an order's own `refund_to` or `payout`. The arbiter's own balance never increased.
- Transaction building for the two-signer `pay` (both auth trees and their signatures) is checked offline against the Soroban authorisation preimage.
- **Not observed:** any testnet behaviour, any fee cost per order, the claimable-balance reserve, or any wallet's `signAuthEntry` support.

## Team assumptions

- **A1.** A custodial PSP can implement the same rules in its own ledger; the chain adds value only for non-custodial PSPs.
- A refund address that signed at checkout removes the exchange-withdrawal failure mode for escrow orders, because an exchange cannot sign `pay`.
- A silent arbiter defaulting to a full refund is acceptable to merchants when the PSP staffs disputes (compare 05's 50/50 default).
- The claimable-balance reserve is one base reserve (0.5 XLM) per claimant, charged to the creator.
- Buyers paying from exchanges will switch to a self-custody wallet for protected orders, or choose a card.

## Hypotheses (to be tested)

- **A2 (the killer question).** Merchants accept a settlement hold of 3–14 days in exchange for recourse, fewer failed refunds and lower fees than cards.
- **H2.** `pay` + `release` cost under $0.01 per order on testnet and mainnet (11-R3's sub-cent claim).
- **H3.** The brief shortens arbiter preparation time per dispute. No baseline exists.
- **H4.** PSPs will pay basis points on protected volume, or a licence.
- **H5.** Wallets used by the target buyers support `signAuthEntry` for the second-wallet refund address. If not, the second wallet signs a separate envelope.

## Simulated / demo data

All of `data/seed/` is generated by `app/src/seed/generate.ts` (fixed seed `20260924`):

- **Merchants.** M1 is a Mexican handicraft exporter (window 14 d, 150 bps, USDC/EURC, express enabled, −06:00). M2 is a Nigerian digital-goods seller (3 d, 125 bps, USDC/PYUSD, +01:00).
- **Orders.** 50 orders over 30 days: M1 has 24 escrow and 6 express, M2 has 20 escrow. By token: USDC 44, EURC 4, PYUSD 2. There are 41 buyers, 3 of whom buy more than once.
  - Amounts are messy: `49.99`, `12.5`, `7.3333333`, `120`, `0.0000050` (the fee rounds to 0), and a `1999.00` outlier.
  - References come in mixed formats (`#1031`, `ORD-1032`, `ord 1044`, `ORD1046 `), and one reference is re-typed so its second `pay` fails with `DuplicateOrder`.
  - One order uses a separate `refund_to` that signs.
- **Events.**
  - Merchant refunds: 3 full and 2 partial. One full refund hits a deauthorised trustline and settles on the keeper's first hourly retry after it is restored two days later.
  - Disputes: D1 is resolved at 40% on day 4 of the dispute. D2 is refunded in full by the merchant during the dispute. D3 times out at `resolve_by`.
  - One late dispute at `T+2h` is rejected with `WindowClosed`.
  - A 26 h keeper outage delays 4 releases.
  - Express: 5 claims after `T` (one attempted 6 h early and refused), and 1 reclaim before `T`.
- **Results** (asserted in `simulate`, `scenario.test.ts` and the Rust scenario test):
  - 38 Released, 4 Refunded (3 outside disputes, 1 inside), 1 Resolved, 1 TimedOut;
  - 35 of 44 escrow orders settle with no action after `pay`;
  - per token, payouts + refunds + fees equal the amount paid, and the contract holds 0 after `withdraw_fees`.
- **Dispute folders.** D1's buyer statement is in Spanish. D3's contains a phone number, a street address, an email and a card number (redaction). The carrier CSV mixes `2026-09-04` and `04/09/2026`. `llm-response.json` in each folder is a hand-written fixture in the Messages API response shape, **not live model output**; D3's is deliberately malformed.
- **Test assets.** "USDC", "EURC" and "PYUSD" in the seed and on testnet are self-issued test assets, not the real stablecoins.

## Experiment plan

**Week 1: 8–10 conversations.**

- **Who.** Non-custodial stablecoin PSPs and checkout providers, including Stellar checkout projects in the community list such as ROZO Checkout (funding status unknown), and 5–6 merchants in Latin America who accept stablecoins.
- **Questions.**
  - Describe your last five stablecoin refund requests: how you got the refund address, how long each took, and whether any failed.
  - What happens today when a buyer and merchant disagree?
  - **Killer question:** would you accept a settlement hold of N days on every order in exchange for one-click refunds, a PSP arbiter and fewer failed refunds? What is the longest N you would accept?
  - PSPs only: do you hold customer funds? A "yes" moves that PSP out of the target market (A1).
- **Stop rule.** If no merchant accepts any hold longer than 24 h, and no non-custodial PSP sees a regulatory or commercial need, stop. Reduce the project to a documented upstream fix proposal for Circle's protocol plus the express-mode library.
- **Output.** Observed baselines from named respondents, with consent.

**Week 2.**

- One merchant's staff run 10–20 testnet orders through the checkout and CLI, with their own policy and window, including at least one refund and one dispute. A PSP person arbitrates the dispute using the brief.
- Measure time per refund and arbiter preparation time with and without the brief, and whether both sides accept the receipt as evidence.
- A PSP product lead reviews `docs/PSP-INTEGRATION.md` and the arbiter bounds; record their objections.
- Before any of this, run `scripts/e2e-testnet.sh` and record the four verify-first results.

**Willingness to pay.** Test basis points on protected volume, with Trustless Work's 0.3% as the visible reference (12-§9), against a flat PSP licence. Record the answers as Observed, with no conversion assumed.

## Killer questions

1. **Would the user care if it disappeared?** Plausible from the researched pain: failed refunds (E2) and no recourse (E3). Untested; demand is unquantified (E7), and custodial PSPs are a real substitute (A1).
2. **Did anyone outside the team use it?** No.
3. **Before/after measured?** No. The baselines (failure rate, time per refund) are unknown even as researched figures; week 1 exists to collect them.
4. **Value lost without AI?** Small and bounded. The contract rules, receipts and express mode work without any model. The heuristic brief alone gives the arbiter a checklist, timeline and inconsistency flags. The LLM adds only a rewritten summary and extra questions, under a numbers guard. The loss without AI is arbiter preparation time (H3), which is unmeasured.
5. **Reason to keep using after the demo?** Every order is protected by default and every refund is one action. For PSPs, the reason is a recourse story for regulators. Hypothesis.
6. **Would someone pay?** Hypothesis: PSPs, through basis points or a licence (H4); merchants indirectly through the PSP fee. No revenue is assumed.
7. **Does the chain create value?** Only for non-custodial PSPs. The chain gives a refund destination nobody can redirect, release after `T` without anyone's approval, an arbiter bounded to one order's two parties, and one record both sides can verify. A custodial PSP's database can enforce the same rules if both sides trust it (A1).
8. **Why this chain?** USDC, EURC and PYUSD are reachable through one SEP-41 code path. The fee floor is 100 stroops per operation. Claimable balances give the no-contract express mode, and anchors serve these merchants' local-currency exits (01-§4, §5, §11). The EVM already has Circle's protocol, so an Arbitrum build would be a fix of it, not a new product.

## Regulatory note

Whether a PSP that arbitrates and takes a fee from a contract escrow is treated as holding client funds, or as offering an escrow service, was not researched. This needs a legal review before any pilot (risk 6 in the specification).
